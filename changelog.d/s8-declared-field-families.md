Classes with public declared fields are born on their layout families: each plain field is a family key in definition order, defined before the constructor body (base) or right after `super()` (derived). Field definitions fill their hole slots as part of the constructor's family run and otherwise keep DefineField semantics (no inherited setter, non-extensible receivers throw); a constructor that only assigns its parameters to its fields is a compact family birth. Classes that store no key of their own take the keys their creation sites store (`const x = new C(); x.k = v`), and stay on the empty birth when none does. Computed-key, private and decorated fields keep the previous construction.
