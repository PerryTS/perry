Performance: two integer-interval looseness causes in the whole-program facts are gone. The result of an interval rule (`a + b`, `r * 16 + f`, a loop test's clip) is now the rule over its operands' intervals and is no longer widened on its own: only copy targets (a loop counter, a `let`) climb the widening ladder and the loop test clips them back, so `r * 16 + f` over two counters in `[0, 7]` is `[0, 119]` instead of `[0, 255]`, and `r + f` is `[0, 14]` instead of `[0, 127]`. A write of a narrowed counter in a branch that always leaves (`if ((sq & 0x88) !== 0) { sq += 7; continue; }`) no longer ends the counter's narrowed reads after the branch, when every way out of the branch lands outside the narrowed code: an unlabeled `break` or `continue` with no loop or `switch` in between, a `return` or `throw` with no `try` in between; a labeled jump, or a write that falls through to a read, keeps the read wide. Field read calls such as `this.at(r * 16 + f)` and `this.at(sq)` on a 0x88 board are then in the board's floor. `PERRY_FACTS_EXACT_RULES=0` and `PERRY_FACTS_LEAVING_WRITES=0` turn each off; `PERRY_FACTS_SABOTAGE=exactrule` and `leavingwrite` are the negative controls.
