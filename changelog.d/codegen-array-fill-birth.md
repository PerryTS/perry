The fill birth: `new Array(k).fill(v)` of an integer literal `k` is born once, in its final representation. The two-step lowering allocated `k` holes (`js_array_constructor_single`), tested the receiver for an own `fill`, called the native `js_array_fill` (every slot written again, then the layout re-derived by reading every slot back) and, for an at-rest Array, `js_array_rest_encode` (every slot rewritten as an I32 word): about 760 instructions for eight zeroes. wpfacts now exports the fused fills that are one birth with their length (`ModuleBindings::fill_births`, probe `# fill birth module=<m> offset=<o> len=<k>`), and codegen lowers such a call as one birth (`expr/fill_birth.rs`): one allocation with the final length and capacity, each element stored once in its final word (the I32 word for an at-rest birth, with the capacity the facts reserve; the value's word otherwise). A small birth (at most sixteen slots) of a plain double or an I32 word is inline (the array literal's bump allocation, header words and one store per slot, no call); every other one calls the new runtime entry `js_array_fill_birth(k, reserve, v, rest)`, which allocates and fills in one pass and takes the two-step path itself for a value that is no Number. The capacity and the layout bits are `perry_abi::fill_birth_capacity` / `fill_birth_layout`, shared by the inline birth and the runtime entry and held to the two-step result by the runtime's tests. Not covered (the two-step birth, as before): a length that is no integer literal, a length past the dense bound, `fill` with a start or an end, a fill on a named Array, a subclass, a program `Array`. Check mode: the born Array has its length and its first and last slot hold the fill word. Probe (`PERRY_SITE_STATS=1`): `PERRY_SITE fill-birth len=<k> cap=<slots> word=<f64|i32|value> form=<inline|call> <function>`. Switch: `PERRY_ARRAY_FILL_BIRTH=0` (no fill birth is exported; a compile-cache input). S0 arm `fillbirth` (an inline birth leaves its last slot a hole). Chess `evaluate` builds two such Arrays per call.
