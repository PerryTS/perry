`new net.Socket({ fd })` adopts the descriptor as the socket's stream and owns it, as under Node; on fds 0 to 2 it leaves the descriptor open when it closes, as libuv does. A readable fd socket starts reading at construction, so data that arrives before the first listener is buffered (`readableLength`) and not lost. `readable: false` and `writable: false` start the socket with that side ended. `end()` on a pipe, FIFO or tty completes after the queued writes drain, with no error, instead of failing the write. The socket emits `prefinish` before the `end()` callbacks and `finish`, write and `end()` callbacks receive `null`, and `closed`, `readableLength`, `readableObjectMode` and `writableObjectMode` are readable.
