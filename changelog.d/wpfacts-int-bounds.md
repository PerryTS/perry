Fix (wrong answer, no error): `let h = 1406932606; h *= h; h *= h; console.log(~h, ~h & 0xffff, ~~h, (~h + 1) | 0)` printed `89915391 65535 -89915392 89915392` (node: `-1 65535 0 0`). The whole-program facts' integer intervals (wpfacts `model::range`) write a bound past ±2^53 as "unbounded" (2^60), but only on the far side: an interval lying wholly past 2^60 kept its exact lower bound beside the unbounded upper one, which reads as the empty interval ("no Number"). So a product such as `1406932606 * 1406932606` (1.98e18) added nothing to the binding it was assigned to, the binding kept the interval of its other values (here the int32 literal), and every int consumer trusted the int32 claim: `~h`, `h | 0`, `h >>> 0`, `h & m`, an element key, `h % n`. The same empty interval made `2147483647 << 31` a non-negative key. Now `Iv::int` gives an interval the bounds of doubles: the near bound of an interval past ±2^53 is ±2^53 itself (the exact integer a rule computes there may round to a neighbouring double), the far one unbounded, never empty; every rule computes its candidate bounds with checked `i64` arithmetic through one helper (`hull`), an overflow is ⊤. A unit test evaluates every rule over every pair of intervals with bounds at the representation boundaries (2^31, 2^32, 2^53, 2^62 and their neighbours) and checks that what the operator computes for doubles of the operands lies in the result. `PERRY_FACTS_INT_BOUNDS=0` restores the old bounds. Check mode (`PERRY_VERIFY_FACTS=1`) reported the false claim as `int32 input` (rc 86) and now passes; S0 control `int-bounds-negative`.

Two more fixes from the same boundary probe (385 generated programs: 64 operator and assignment forms over 32 boundary values, at module level, in a function, a loop, a closure, a parameter and a field). (1) An `i32` shift lowered inside a native int32 chain shifted by the count operand itself: `let v = -1; v = 1 << v;` (or `x <<= k` with a variable `k` of 33) was an LLVM shift by a count past the width, poison: the value was garbage, and the program crashed or looped. The chains now mask the count to five bits like the other shift lowerings (`LlBlock::js_shift_count`; `PERRY_SHIFT_COUNT_MASK=0` restores). (2) The literal `9223372036854775808` (2^63) was lowered as the i64 integer 2^63 - 1 (`as i64` saturates; the range test compared against `i64::MAX as f64`, which is 2^63), so `x | 0` of it folded to -1 instead of 0; it is a Number literal now.
