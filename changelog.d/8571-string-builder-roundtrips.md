**Resolve the Rust-buffer round trips audited in #8434 without trading simplicity for regressions.** `String.prototype.repeat` now computes its exact UTF-16/byte size, roots and re-reads the receiver after the single GC allocation, and fills the destination with exponentially growing bulk copies. On the 64 B–1 MiB sweep this cuts wall time from 1.095 s to 439.8 ms (2.49x faster) and retired instructions from roughly 13.90B to 6.98B (49.8% fewer).

Direct-write candidates were also implemented and measured for `Array.prototype.join`, `padStart`/`padEnd`, and literal/regex/callback replace. They were deliberately not retained: join was 6.83x slower, pad retired about 13% more instructions despite a small wall-time improvement, and replace remained about 12% slower with about 11% more instructions. Join keeps the capacity-hinted buffered path already on `main`; pad and replace keep their simpler buffered builders.

Those buffered paths now finalize through UTF-16/WTF-8-aware construction, preserve lone-surrogate metadata, and canonicalize surrogate pairs created at join/pad/replace boundaries. Empty literal-pattern replacement now iterates JavaScript UTF-16 boundaries (including between an astral character's surrogate halves), and no-match replace copies remain moving-GC safe. Node 26.5.1 parity covers holes, empty separators/elements, astrals, lone surrogates, allocating join coercions and replace callbacks, named/fancy regex, and forced evacuation verification.
