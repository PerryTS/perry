Whole-program facts decide the birth capacity of a push-grown list: a `let x = []` that a `push` in a loop entered after the birth can reach (in its own function, or in a callee, closure or method that loops and pushes into it) is born with 40 slots instead of 4 (or the 16 codegen's own loop collector gave a local pushed in a loop of its body), so a list of up to 40 elements never regrows; a list reached only by straight-line pushes of its own function is born with exactly as many slots as they can append (above 4, at most 40). The capacity is not observable and no proof is consumed: a push past it grows the storage as before. `PERRY_LIST_BIRTH_CAPACITY=0` restores the previous births, `=<n>` (5..=64) sets the cap; the facts dump has one `# list_cap` line per `[]` binding.
