Runtime layouts name their prototypes the way `Object.create(P)` families do: through a prototype family whose serial is the prototype's identity, here a reserved intrinsic serial fixed in the binary. An object born on such a layout has its prototype without a link and shares its shapes with `Object.create(P)` receivers. A sealed runtime prototype (URL, Timeout, Immediate) also gets its own shadow bit set by any write after the runtime built it. The per-registration prototype identities, their builtin tags and the separate binding call are gone.
