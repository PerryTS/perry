Performance: small strings are built at the site. Three results that are an inline (SSO) string were still paid for as general string work. (1) `a + b` of two strings called `js_string_concat_box` (about 64 instructions through its short-pair arm); the site now tests the operands itself (both inline, both ASCII, at most five bytes together) and builds the result from their bits, the same bits the runtime's arm builds; every other pair takes the call unchanged. (2) `String(n)`, `` `${n}` ``, `n.toString()` and `"" + n` of a one-digit integer went through the five-digit fixed-point conversion (about 55 instructions); an integral `0 <= n <= 9` is now the digit's byte or'ed into a constant, and the test folds away where LLVM knows the range. (3) `s.charAt(i)` as a direct operand of a string `+` answers the character as an inline string in its inline arms (the heap one-character string could not be joined at the site); the slow arm is the entry call as before. All three are chosen by tests on the run-time value, so there is no proof and nothing for check mode to witness.
