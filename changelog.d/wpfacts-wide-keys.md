Performance: an element read or store whose key the whole-program facts prove a non-negative integer Number that may lie past the int32 range (a counter bounded below by its loop test only, such as the `j` of an insertion sort, or a `>>> 0` result) no longer calls the shared key normalizer. wpfacts exports such sites as `wide_keys` (the hull of the key over every copy of the site; disjoint from `key_signs`); codegen clamps the key to 2^32 - 1 and converts it unsigned, two instructions, and the element tier's one unsigned bound decides. A key at or past 2^31 leaves the tier on the arm that holds the original value, so an index up to 2^32 - 2 and a named property at or past 2^32 - 1 are read and written exactly as before; a key that may be negative, a fraction, NaN, `-0`, a string or a symbol has no such interval and keeps the normalizer. Check mode (`PERRY_VERIFY_FACTS=1`) tests the claim at each consuming site and reports a false proof as "wide key" (exit 86); `PERRY_FACTS_SABOTAGE=widekey` is its negative control. `PERRY_FACTS_WIDE_KEYS=0` turns the facts and their consumers off; the dump (`PERRY_FACTS_DUMP`) marks such a site `wide=1` on its `# key_sign` line.
