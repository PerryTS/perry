The module walk prepares module sources ahead of itself on up to four worker threads. Reading a file, the CommonJS wrap, the static-require transform and the scans over their result depend only on the file and on settings fixed before the walk, so workers prepare each module the walk has queued, plus the relative imports they find in prepared sources, while the walk lowers earlier modules. The walk consumes results strictly in its own order. It checks that each result was prepared for the classification it computes itself, and recomputes inline otherwise. Context updates, errors and diagnostics (computed-require warnings, parse warnings, `PERRY_DEBUG_CJS_WRAP`) are carried in the result and applied or printed by the walk, so output and stderr order match a serial walk. `CompilationContext::source_prep_workers = Some(0)` forces the serial walk, and a test checks that both walks collect identical modules.

The CommonJS wrap runs `extract_exports_from_source`, the require-specifier scan and the re-export scans once per module instead of up to three times. The cycle check shares, across a module's specifiers, the files already shown not to reach the module, instead of re-reading the require graph once per specifier. Regex scans for `__exportStar(require(…))`, `exports.`, `exports[`, `defineProperty`, `__export` and `createRequire` are skipped when the source lacks the text every match needs.

Next.js standalone: the front end goes from about 87–91 s to 52 s, and source preparation drops out of the walk's serial path. Module objects are byte-identical to the previous build.
